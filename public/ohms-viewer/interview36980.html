Segment Synopsis: PXA discusses Trần Quốc Bửu's journey as a politically flexible figure shaped by overlapping foreign influences in Vietnam during the mid-20th century. He initially worked with the Việt Minh and received Japanese training alongside leaders like Nguyễn Bình and Trình Minh Thế. Later, French intelligence recruited him to lead anti-communist labor organizations, countering worker mobilization by communists. In the early 1950s, he cooperated with Ngô Đình Diệm and joined the Cần Lao party under Ngô Đình Nhu’s influence. Despite these alliances with both French and South Vietnamese authorities, his relationship with Diệm’s regime ultimately proved short-lived and unstable.

Segment Synopsis: In 1955–56, Ngô Đình Diệm intensified authoritarian control by abolishing traditional village elections and suppressing opposition, including nationalist and religious groups like the Cao Đài and Hòa Hảo, despite their anti-communist stance. This caused widespread dissatisfaction and strikes, particularly among villagers. At the same time, Dr. Trần Kim Tuyến, a trusted political operative under Ngô Đình Diệm, led the Political, Social, and Cultural Office, which included secret police functions despite his limited formal intelligence training. To prevent communist influence, he deliberately split up the Office as well as made a new labor organization, ensuring mutual surveillance and control. The office had previously been headed by Vũ Tiến Huân, a French-trained administrator and Diệm ally. By late 1956, Tuyến replaced Huân as chief of the Office, following reorganizations. PXA then shifted to discuss Dr. Tuyến's background. He is someone who is more to the Catholic Church than personally to the Ngô family, with a background in religion and literature as well as partial medical education disrupted by French military conscription.
