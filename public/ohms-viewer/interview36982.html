Direct segment link:
interview36982.html#segment2480

Partial Transcript: Look at My Lai. Not all of them, like, behave like Mr. Calley.

Segment Synopsis: Phan Xuan An sits back down and they begin a brief discussion about the My Lai Massacre and its nuances and legacy.
He shifts from talking about how hatred is conditioned to giving general musings about life, discussing how life is controlled a lot by fate. He tells a story about a former classmate who had a bright future, but her life became unhappy because her husband became addicted to drugs and their marriage failed. He uses this story to show that even educated and privileged people can suffer because of destiny. He briefly talks about his own life during the war. Many cadres and people were killed or captured, but he survived. He believes this was partly because of protection and fate; he mentions being protected by a “goddess.” He also speaks about Americans and reconciliation after the war. He suggests that ordinary people can get along despite past conflicts and that understanding between cultures is possible.
Overall, he reflects on fate, survival, suffering, and the possibility of reconciliation after war.
